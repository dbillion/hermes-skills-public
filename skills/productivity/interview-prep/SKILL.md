# Interview Prep

Prepare for job interviews by researching the company, generating STAR-format stories, drafting application answers, and creating follow-up plans.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Company Research

Research the company across multiple sources in parallel:

1. **Web search** for recent news, product launches, tech stack, funding
2. **GitHub org** — check their open-source work, tech languages, recent activity
3. **Engineering blog** — architecture decisions, scale challenges, team structure
4. **Job posting** — extract required skills, responsibilities, team description
5. **LinkedIn** — employee count, recent hires, company culture signals

Synthesize into a structured brief covering:
- What they do (product, mission, scale)
- Tech stack (languages, frameworks, infrastructure)
- Team structure and the specific role's scope
- Recent news / strategic direction
- Competitors and market position

### Phase 2: Skills Match Matrix

Create a table mapping the job requirements to your experience:

| Requirement | Your Fit | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| (from job posting) | ✅/⚠️/❌ | Specific project or achievement |

Identify gaps and prepare honest responses for them.

### Phase 3: STAR Stories

Generate 5-7 stories from the candidate's experience using the STAR format:

- **Situation:** Context (1-2 sentences)
- **Task:** What needed to happen (1 sentence)
- **Action:** What you did (2-3 sentences, specific technical details)
- **Result:** Quantified outcome (1 sentence with numbers)

**Categories to cover:**
1. Owning a system end-to-end (leadership + ownership)
2. Building something from scratch (greenfield / AI systems)
3. Performance optimization at scale (backend + GCP)
4. Cross-functional collaboration (teamwork)
5. Learning fast under pressure (adaptability)
6. Hard technical problem-solving (debugging / architecture)
7. Cost optimization / efficiency (cloud spend, token reduction)

**Rules:**
- Each story must have **quantified results** (%, ms, $)
- Stories should be **specific** — name the tech, the scale, the outcome
- Avoid generic "I worked on X" — show **your** contribution
- Prepare 2-3 backup stories in case some don't come up

### Phase 4: Application Answers

Draft answers for common application questions:

- "Tell me about yourself" → 60-second pitch: current role + key achievement + why this company
- "Why [company]?" → Tie their mission/tech to your experience + specific projects
- "Hard technical problem" → Pick the strongest STAR story, go deep on the technical details
- "Where do you see yourself in 2 years?" → Growth trajectory aligned with their team

**Tone guidelines:**
- Conversational, not scripted
- Show genuine enthusiasm (specific details about their product)
- Keep answers under 2 minutes when spoken

### Phase 5: Questions to Ask Them

Prepare 5 strategic questions:
1. Team structure and ownership scope
2. Current biggest technical challenge
3. First 90 days expectations
4. Engineering culture (code review, deployment frequency, on-call)
5. Growth path for the role

### Phase 6: Follow-Up Plan

After the interview:
- Send thank-you message within 24 hours
- Reference a specific conversation topic
- Reiterate interest with one concrete value-add

## Output Format

Deliver via Telegram or file:
1. Company brief (structured, 1 page)
2. Skills match matrix (table)
3. STAR stories (numbered, with categories)
4. Application answers (question → answer pairs)
5. Questions to ask (numbered)
6. Follow-up template

## Pitfalls

- **Don't fabricate experience** — if you don't have a story for a category, say so and prepare a transferable skill instead
- **Don't be generic** — "I'm a fast learner" is weak. "I ramped up on telecom systems at Ericsson in 4 weeks and shipped X" is strong
- **Don't forget the "why"** — always connect your story back to why it matters for their specific role
- **Don't skip company research** — generic answers signal low interest. Reference their specific tech, product, or recent news.
