# Paper Structure Templates

## General Research Paper (2000-3000 words)

### Title Page
- Title: Clear, specific, no jargon
- Your name
- Course name and code
- Instructor name
- Date

### Introduction (~200-300 words)
```
HOOK: Start with a question, story, or striking observation
[Not: "This paper will discuss..." | Instead: jump straight into the topic]

CONTEXT: 2-3 sentences setting up the scene
- What's the issue or question?
- Why does it matter?

THESIS: 1-2 sentences stating your argument
- Clear, specific claim you'll defend

ROADMAP: 1-2 sentences previewing your sections
- "First, I will... Then... Finally..."
```

### Body Section 1: Background/Context (~400-500 words)
```
HEADING: Descriptive, specific [not "Background" but "The Problem of Evil in Modern Theology"]

CLAIM: What does this section argue?
EVIDENCE: Source quotes (short, 1-2 sentences max)
ANALYSIS: YOUR words explaining what the evidence means
TRANSITION: Bridge to next section
```

### Body Section 2: Main Argument (~500-700 words)
```
HEADING: [Specific to your argument]

CLAIM: Your main point
EVIDENCE: 2-3 supporting sources with citations
ANALYSIS: The longest part — your own thinking
PERSONAL: How this connects to ministry/church life
```

### Body Section 3: Counter-Arguments (~400-500 words)
```
HEADING: ["But What About...?" or specific counterpoint]

CLAIM: What do others argue against your position?
EVIDENCE: Show you understand opposing views fairly
RESPONSE: Why your position still holds (or how it's nuanced)
```

### Conclusion (~200-300 words)
```
SUMMARY: Briefly restate your thesis and main points
[Not: "In conclusion, this paper has shown..." | Instead: just restate]

SIGNIFICANCE: Why does this matter for the church today?
PERSONAL: What did you learn? How has your thinking changed?
FORWARD: What questions remain? What should the church do?
```

## Exegetical Paper (1500-2500 words)

### Introduction
- State the passage being examined
- Brief context (book, author, audience)
- Thesis: what the passage means and why it matters

### Exegesis
```
GRAMMAR/SYNTAX: What do the words mean in original language?
- Short quotes from commentaries
- Your analysis of key terms

HISTORICAL CONTEXT: What was happening when this was written?
- Who was the audience?
- What problem was being addressed?

LITERARY CONTEXT: Where does this fit in the larger book?
- How does it connect to what comes before/after?

THEOLOGICAL MEANING: What does this teach about God?
- What's the main theological point?
- How does this connect to the broader biblical story?
```

### Application
```
For the original audience: What did this mean for them?
For today's church: What does this mean for us?
Personal: How does this challenge or encourage you?
```

## Systematic Theology Paper (2500-4000 words)

### Introduction
- Define the doctrine/topic
- State why it's important
- Thesis: your position on the issue

### Biblical Foundation
- Key passages examined
- How Scripture addresses this doctrine
- Your exegesis (not just proof-texting)

### Historical Development
- How has the church understood this over time?
- Key theologians and their contributions
- Where disagreements exist

### Theological Analysis
- Your argument for a particular position
- Engagement with multiple viewpoints
- Evidence from Scripture, tradition, reason

### Contemporary Application
- How does this doctrine affect the church today?
- Practical implications for ministry
- Personal reflection

## Ministry/Practical Theology Paper (2000-3000 words)

### Introduction
- Describe the ministry situation or problem
- Why is this important for the church?
- Thesis: your approach or recommendation

### Biblical/Theological Basis
- What does Scripture say about this?
- Theological framework for your approach

### Current Practice
- How is the church currently handling this?
- What's working? What's not?
- Evidence from ministry experience

### Proposed Approach
- What should be done differently?
- Why will this work better?
- Practical steps for implementation

### Evaluation
- How will you know if it works?
- What are the potential challenges?
- How does this connect to the gospel?

## Formatting Rules

1. **Font**: Times New Roman, 12pt
2. **Spacing**: Double-spaced throughout
3. **Margins**: 1 inch all sides
4. **Page numbers**: Top right corner
5. **Headings**: Bold, descriptive, sentence case (not Title Case)
6. **Paragraphs**: First line indent 0.5"
7. **Block quotes**: 4+ lines, indented 0.5", no quotation marks
8. **Bible references**: In-text, not in reference list
9. **Reference list**: New page, "References" centered at top, alphabetical, hanging indent
