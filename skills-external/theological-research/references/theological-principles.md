│ ### Core Research Principles                                                                             │
│                                                                                                          │
│ According to the **Student Handbook**, a research paper is an academic work designed to **objectively    │
│ discover an answer** to a specific question or problem [1]. Key principles include:                      │
│                                                                                                          │
│ *   **Student Ownership:** Approximately **80% of the paper must be in the student’s own words**,        │
│ explaining and critiquing various positions [1].                                                         │
│ *   **Use of Experts:** Students should use **short quotes** (rarely exceeding 2-3 sentences) from       │
│ expert authors to explain, defend, or contrast the positions being developed [1].                        │
│ *   **Logical Structure:** Papers must follow a determined structure: a clear **introduction of the      │
│ research question**, interaction with **diverse authors and opinions**, and a logical analysis leading   │
│ to a conclusion [2].                                                                                     │
│ *   **Source Quality and Quantity:** A minimum of **five sources** is required in the bibliography, in   │
│ addition to the Bible and lecture notes [3]. At least **three must be originally printed sources**       │
│ (books or journals), and two should be in English if possible [3]. Students are encouraged to use        │
│ **seminary journals and Google Scholar** while avoiding Wikipedia and personal blogs [4].                │
│ *   **Academic Integrity:** Proper referencing is essential to distinguish student ideas from borrowed   │
│ facts; **failure to provide proper referencing results in automatic failure** [2, 5]. The use of AI      │
│ tools like ChatGPT is considered academic fraud unless specifically allowed and clearly referenced [6].  │
│                                                                                                          │
│ ### Formatting Requirements                                                                              │
│                                                                                                          │
│ Research must adhere strictly to **APA 7 format** for both in-text citations and the bibliography [4,    │
│ 7]. Specific requirements include:                                                                       │
│                                                                                                          │
│ *   **Author-Date Citations:** Use the (Author, Year) format and **avoid numbered in-text citations**    │
│ [4, 7].                                                                                                  │
│ *   **Page Numbers:** When citing specific information, include page numbers: **(Smith, 2023, p. 45)**   │
│ or **(Smith, 2023, pp. 45-47)** for multiple pages [7-9].                                                │
│ *   **Bibliography Structure:** References should follow the format: *Author, A. A. (Year). Title of     │
│ article. Title of Journal, Volume(Issue), pages. DOI or URL* [7].                                        │
│ *   **Technical Standards:** Formatting must be "excellent" to achieve the highest grades, while         │
│ significant deviations can lead to lower marks [10, 11].                                                 │
│                                                                                                          │
│ ### Stylistic Guidance                                                                                   │
│                                                                                                          │
│ The sources emphasize a writing style that balances academic rigor with a **personal, conversational     │
│ tone** [4, 7].                                                                                           │
│                                                                                                          │
│ *   **"Real Talk" and Simplicity:** Write as if sitting with someone over coffee, using **simple words   │
│ and short sentences** rather than "fancy" vocabulary [4, 7, 12].                                         │
│ *   **Engaging the Reader:** Use **questions that interrupt the flow** to make the reader think, such as │
│ "But why does this matter?" or "Have you ever felt this way?" [7, 12, 13].                               │
│ *   **Narrative and Humor:** Incorporate **stories from life and ministry**, real examples from church   │
│ life, and even humor to make the content stick [7, 12, 14, 15].                                          │
│ *   **Personal Integration:** Include **personal prayer moments** and reflections on how the experiences │
│ of historical figures or the nuances of a topic reflect your own faith journey [7, 13, 16, 17].          │
│ *   **Organizational Clarity:** Use **clear, descriptive headings** to guide the reader through the      │
│ transitions of the paper (e.g., "The Big Shift" or "The Perils of Respectability") [13, 18, 19].         │
│ *   **Contemporary Application:** Always seek to connect the research to the **modern church context**   │
│ or personal faith, asking how the legacy of the subject still influences believers today [20-22].        │
